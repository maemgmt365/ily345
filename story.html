the story

this didn't start as a brand.

it started from something real.

someone once shared the idea of 345 with me through something simple: the more things you say yes to, the more things you have to say no to.

when you're building something, chasing something, or just locked in on what you want, sometimes the people you care about get less of you. not because you don't care. just because you're focused.

for me, that showed up in missed conversations, short responses, and not always being present the way i wanted to be.

so instead of leaving it there, i'd just send 345. a simple way of saying i'm here, i'm thinking about you, and i still mean these words.

over time, this became something deeper for me.

it was how my mom and i stayed connected. a simple way to say what mattered, even when conversations weren't perfect.

after she passed, those words carried more weight.

it made me more intentional about not leaving things unsaid.

whether in absence or in abundance, these are words we don't hear enough.

the part about saying it out loud

here is the honest version. i have people in my life i can tell i love them. good, close friends. i'm lucky in that. and there are still people i'm close with that i won't say "i love you" to. i'll say 3 4 5. it's easier to get out. it does the same job.

i don't think that's just me. most of us say it freely as kids and then somewhere after we leave home it gets quiet. with men it's more obvious. it reads as soft, or vulnerable, or like a bigger deal than it needs to be. so it goes unsaid, for years, between people who absolutely mean it.

what the surveys show
kids and parents
1 in 5

adults tell their mom they love her every day. 16% say they rarely do, and 80% feel they should say it more.

families
45%

of men would be uncomfortable telling a male relative, a dad or a brother, that they love him. for women with a female relative it is 17%.

men
15%

of american men say they have no close friends at all, up from 3% in 1990. the share with six or more close friends fell by half.

women
7%

of women and 8% of men would be uncomfortable saying it to a partner. with family and friends the number climbs fast. it is not the feeling that is missing, it is the moment.

friends
31%

of gen z men think men should not say "i love you" to their friends. among boomer men it is 20%. the youngest generation is the most guarded.

teenagers
daily

in a 21-day study, teens felt most loved on the days their parents showed warmth, and it reset every day. the researchers' line: love has to be made and remade daily.

that's the whole purpose of this. not to replace the words. to make it easier to say them until you can say the real thing. a hat is a low-stakes way to tell your brother you're proud of him. a hoodie on your dad says it every time he puts it on. the numbers do the talking until you're ready to.

this starts loud. eventually, 345 becomes something you don't have to explain.
